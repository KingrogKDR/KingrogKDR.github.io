Attention is infrastructure

March 30, 2026 · 1 min read

What infrastructure means

Infrastructure is the stuff that everything else depends on. Roads. Water. Electrical grids. We invest in it collectively because we understand, intuitively, that without it, nothing else works. Individual effort cannot substitute for it.

I want to make the case that attention, whether individual or collective, is infrastructure. And that we are currently allowing it to be strip-mined, wasting it on tasks that do not matter. We have become cattle, consuming whatever the corporations are feeding us.

Why deep thought is downstream of attention

The argument is simple: most valuable things that humans make require sustained, uninterrupted thought. Good writing, good software, good research, good decisions - all of these are downstream of the ability to hold a problem in mind long enough to actually understand it.

When attention is fragmented - by notifications, by feeds, by the low-grade anxiety of always being reachable - that capacity degrades. Not dramatically, not all at once, but steadily. Like a road that isn't maintained.

The collective consequence

The difference between infrastructure and private resources is that infrastructure has collective consequences. Your inattention is your problem. But a culture of inattention - a set of tools and norms that systematically fragments focus - is everyone's problem.

I don't have know a solution for this. I have, instead, a reframe: think of your attention as something worth protecting not just for your own productivity, but because the quality of what gets built and thought and decided in the world depends on whether enough people can still think deeply.
