On slowness as a creative practice

March 29, 2026

The anxiety of constant output

There is a particular kind of anxiety that comes with the modern creative life: the feeling that if you are not producing, you are falling behind. Every day without a post, a tweet, a shipped feature, is a day lost. The feed moves on without you.

I've been trying, deliberately, to move slower. Not out of laziness (honestly, I've never worked harder before) but out of a suspicion that the pressure to constantly output is quietly destroying the conditions that make good work possible.

Speed optimizes for volume. Slowness optimizes for depth. And depth is what I actually care about.

When I gave myself permission to sit with an idea for weeks instead of days, I was finally able to understand it well. The idea stopped being a thing to be processed or consumed and became a thing to be understood. I started noticing what I actually thought, rather than what I was supposed to think.

Protecting the slow

This is not an argument against shipping, or publishing, or being present online. It's an argument for protecting some portion of your creative life from the logic of throughput. For letting some things be slow.

The best work I've done and will do from now on, the pieces I'm most proud of, the projects that held together - all came from periods where I resisted the urge to publish too soon. The silence before the task is as much a part of the work as the task itself.
